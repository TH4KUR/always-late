\begin{slide}{Completed evidence}
\begin{itemize}
\item Selected language-feature groups have \key{measurable next-token effects} beyond activity-matched controls in all 27 languages of both Gemma sizes.
\item \key{Better quantization improves preservation and transfer}. GPTQ reduces the changes seen under the original RTN recipe.
\item Activation agreement, language association and intervention effects provide \key{different measurements}.
\item The paper reports positive effects alongside a comprehension null and limited repair. Most analyses are exploratory.
\end{itemize}
\source{Course report revision 4; reports/FINAL\_RESEARCH\_QUESTIONS.md}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{Final synthesis questions}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.6em]
\item How do selectivity and resource tier relate to activation preservation across quantizers?
\item What do language-group interventions change across tasks and precision?
\item How informative are activation survival and association retention about intervention effects?
\item How do context, downstream weights and generic sensitivity affect intervention transfer?
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{0.3em}
These organize the completed work retrospectively. Original frozen hypotheses and verdicts remain part of the record.
\source{reports/FINAL\_RESEARCH\_QUESTIONS.md}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{Models, text and interventions}
\begin{itemize}
\item Primary: Gemma-2-2B, layer 12, Gemma Scope SAE with 16,384 features.
\item Extensions: Gemma-2-9B with its layer-20 Gemma Scope SAE; Llama-3.2-1B with a TopK SAE.
\item FLORES: 27 languages, 300 aligned evaluation sentences each. Belebele: 18 languages, 385 questions each. Fresh Wikipedia transfer panel: six languages.
\item Remove or scale the top 64 selected features; compare with activity-matched non-selective groups. Preserve the SAE reconstruction error.
\item Model inference bfloat16, SAE and edits float32. Sham and weight-restore identities check the edit path.
\end{itemize}
\source{Functional, transfer and 9B freezes; course report Sections 3--4}

\begin{slide}{RQ1: Selectivity and resource tier}
\begin{itemize}
\item Original RTN selective-minus-control survival: \key{\(-1.44\) percentage points at INT6}, Holm-adjusted \(p=0.128\). Non-rejection does not establish equivalence.
\item INT6 survival for low/mid/high tiers: \key{69.4/90.6/95.1\%}. Conditioning on activation scale attenuates the initial tier association.
\item Matched RTN changes the significance label of small contrasts; GPTQ shows no selective penalty in those tests.
\item Preservation depends on the quantizer and feature activity. Resource tiers are observational categories.
\end{itemize}
\source{reports/phase1\_summary.md; reports/gptq\_extension/results/stage1/}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{What the survival correlation measures}
An exact least-squares identity separates gain, noise and signal scale:
\[
 b=ga+c+e,\qquad
 \lambda=\log(R_1^{-2}-1)=\log\sigma_e^2-2\log|g|-2\log\sigma_a.
\]
\begin{itemize}
\item Streamed statistics cover \key{214,506 active language--feature cells}; correlation reproduction within \(3\times10^{-8}\).
\item Across six cells, scale correlation is about \key{\(-0.51\) for \(\lambda\)}, versus \key{\(-0.004\) to \(+0.114\)} for threshold-normalized encoder perturbation \(\nu\).
\item This clarifies activation preservation. It does not imply that correlation or SAEs provide no information.
\end{itemize}
\source{reports/survival\_measurement/stage3/stage3\_results.md}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{RQ2: Selected groups matter for predicting text}
\begin{itemize}
\item Gemma-2-2B FP excess ablation harm is positive with a lower interval above zero in \key{27 of 27 languages}.
\item Median excess harm: \key{0.262 nats/token}. Mean: 0.704 [0.684,0.723], with sentence-bootstrap uncertainty.
\item At INT6 the effect stays near FP. At INT4 the median per-language effect ratio is \key{1.54 under legacy RTN}, 1.36 under matched RTN, \key{1.22 under GPTQ}.
\item These are causal effects of defined edits. A larger removal effect measures changed dependence on the group.
\end{itemize}
\source{Functional results/eval/report.md; GPTQ stage3 analysis\_gptq\_eval/report.md}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{Graded suppression and limited gain repair}
\begin{itemize}
\item Deeper suppression increases net harm in \key{all 27 languages}. FP median effects: 0.022, 0.090, 0.169, 0.262 nats/token.
\item Dose-response and consistency rules hold; the strict legacy precision-ordering rule fails because of an intermediate-width dip.
\item Main fine-gain repair criteria fail. A fresh-sentence RTN follow-up finds a small \(1.1\times\) benefit, about \key{0.00135 nats/token} (20/27 languages).
\item The main 300-sentence boosting hint weakens under GPTQ. State-defined groups are not more consequential, and shrinkage does not predict the optimal gain.
\end{itemize}
\source{reports/functional\_validation/gain\_study/; reports/gptq\_extension/RESULTS.md}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{The Llama check shows heterogeneity}
\begin{itemize}
\item Llama-3.2-1B FP median excess harm is \key{0.048 [0.018,0.173]} nats/token, positive in 21/27 languages.
\item Its frozen consistency rule requires 24/27, so the recorded verdict is \key{not consistent}.
\item The INT4 median effect ratio is \key{0.86 [0.74,1.03]}; the Gemma amplification pattern does not carry over.
\item Model family, dictionary, depth, size and tokenizer change together. The discrepancy bounds generalization and preserves the measured Gemma contrasts.
\end{itemize}
\source{reports/functional\_validation/llama/report.md; llama\_robust/}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{Comprehension and patch recovery measure different outcomes}
\begin{itemize}
\item Belebele FP excess correct-option loss: \key{\(-0.0001\) nats/item [\(-0.0035,+0.0031\)]}. The required FP effect is not established.
\item Accuracy is a separate endpoint: about 42.6\% FP. GPTQ INT4 reaches 31.8\%, above 25\% chance, with a small group-ablation change.
\item Non-selective control sets reduce accuracy by 1.2--2.2 points; other-language selective controls reach 6.0 points.
\item INT4 donor recovery: selected K64 group \key{2--3\% of the loss gap}, full residual \key{35--41\%}. These patch recovery shares do not partition causal responsibility.
\end{itemize}
\source{Functional mc\_eval/report.md, rescue/report.md; GPTQ stage3/}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{Activation survival adds modest positive predictive information}
\begin{itemize}
\item On 1,412 reliable features, adding continuous activation correlation improves language-held-out effect prediction by \key{3.1--4.4\%} across all six Gemma cells.
\item Every 95\% language-bootstrap interval is above zero. Legacy INT4: \key{3.7\% [2.3,5.2]}; GPTQ INT4: \key{3.5\% [2.1,5.0]}.
\item These positive gains fall below the project's 10\% practical convention. Gain and \(\nu\) provide no resolved additional improvement.
\item Llama INT4 repeats a modest contribution: \key{4.2\% [1.6,6.7]}. Binary association labels are less informative.
\end{itemize}
\source{reports/survival\_effect\_rescue/stage4\_gemma\_report\_2026-10-28.md; llama/}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{RQ4: Crossed context and downstream weights}
For the same FP-amplitude edit, compare four model states:
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.3}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
 & FP suffix & Quantized suffix\\
\midrule
FP prefix & \(D_{FF}\) & \(D_{FQ}\)\\
Quantized prefix & \(D_{QF}\) & \(D_{QQ}\)\\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\par
\end{center}
\[
 C=D_{QF}-D_{FF},\quad W=D_{FQ}-D_{FF},\quad
 I=D_{QQ}-D_{QF}-D_{FQ}+D_{FF}.
\]
The vector identity is exact. Term norms and medians need not add. A fifth cell uses the quantized model's own amplitude.
\source{reports/intervention\_transfer/results/main/; course report Section 8}

\begin{slide}{Prediction and fresh-text transfer}
\begin{itemize}
\item Generic sensitivity reduces main-panel INT4 prediction error by \key{21.5\% [12.7,29.4]}. INT6: 10.9\% [\(-1.2\),21.4], unresolved.
\item A similar predictor works at INT16. Its value concerns generic intervention sensitivity, beyond quantization alone.
\item Fresh Wikipedia INT4 effect cosine: \key{0.754/0.808/0.876} for legacy/matched/GPTQ. The quantizer ordering repeats.
\item External prediction criteria hold at both widths for RTN; GPTQ meets INT4 but not INT6. Averaging fingerprints raises apparent transfer agreement.
\end{itemize}
\source{Transfer predictive\_validation.csv; GPTQ RESULTS.md, external panel}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{Interpretation and shared methodological limits}
\begin{itemize}
\item SAE features depend on dictionary training, sparsity and size. Incomplete recovery and mixed features remain open field-wide issues.
\item Defined interventions yield causal effects in the tested model. A complete natural circuit account requires additional evidence.
\item Most analyses reuse previously examined sentences. Frozen stage choices improve transparency without creating independent confirmation.
\item Fixed model/layer/dictionary settings bound external validity. Quantizer comparisons and the 9B/Llama checks already provide useful evidence about that boundary.
\end{itemize}
\source{Gao et al.\ (2024); Gemma Scope (2024); course report limitations}
\end{slide}

\begin{slide}{Answers to the four synthesis questions}
\begin{enumerate}[itemsep=0.6em]
\item \key{Preservation depends on quantizer and scale}. A universal selective-destruction account is not established.
\item \key{Groups contribute to text prediction}. Behavioral relevance varies by task and edit; main gain repair remains limited.
\item \key{Activation and effect preservation share modest information}. Recognizable functional identity survives substantial activation drift.
\item \key{Portability is measurable and quantizer-dependent}. Context/weight contrasts and generic controls clarify where transfers change.
\end{enumerate}
\source{reports/FINAL\_RESEARCH\_QUESTIONS.md; completed-study manuscript}
\end{slide}
