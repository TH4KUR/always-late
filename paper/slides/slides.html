Research questions

Four questions, from activation to effect

RQ1
Which language features retain their activation patterns after quantization?
Measured by activation correlation $R_1$
RQ2
How does editing language features affect text prediction and comprehension?
Measured by excess harm, dose-response and Belebele accuracy
RQ3
How well do activation measures predict whether a feature’s output effect is preserved?
Measured by effect cosine $W_1$, swap control, patching
RQ4
How much does the same feature edit change when the model is quantized?
Measured by context, later-weight and interaction terms

$E_\ell$ is the excess harm for language $\ell$ in nats per token; positive means the language edit hurts more than the control edit

Limitations

What these results do not show

01
One layer, one SAE
Language-specific units concentrate in early and late layers; we tested a middle one
02
Fixed-length inputs
Truncation is controlled across precisions but untested with complete sentences
03
Mostly exploratory
Most FLORES analyses are exploratory; only Belebele was confirmatory
04
Bounded to Gemma
The Llama inconsistency limits the finding to the Gemma family
05
Simulated quantization
Weights are rounded in floating point, not run with integer kernels
Claims

What we claim

1
Language features help next-word prediction in all 27 languages, on both Gemma sizes
2
Dose-response confirmed in every language
3
Activation survival understates preservation: 13% “survive”, 99.7% still work
4
The 4-bit drift is generic (direction-swap control), and restoring features repairs only 2.6%
5
Activation agreement, task performance and effect need separate measurements
