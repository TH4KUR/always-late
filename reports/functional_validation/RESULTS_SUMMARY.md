# Functional validation across precision: results summary for the paper team

Written 2026-10-23 (Claude). This page collects, per question, what was done, whether the result is frozen/confirmatory or exploratory, the headline numbers and what they do and do not show. It is a map, not a source: the numbers come from the files linked in each row (every number of section 2 was re-checked against its source file on 2026-10-23, see the Log of [`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md)), the dated log and the validation of every job are in [`STATUS.md`](STATUS.md) (Log; the entries of 2026-10-21 and 2026-10-22 are in [`../archive/status_log_2026-10-21_to_22.md`](../archive/status_log_2026-10-21_to_22.md)), the readings in [`../decisions_and_anomalies.md`](../decisions_and_anomalies.md) (D-041 to D-066), the open choices in [`../../DECISIONS_FOR_REVIEW.md`](../../DECISIONS_FOR_REVIEW.md). The team writes the paper's prose (`paper/` is never edited by this routine). **Status words:** *frozen* = the setting and the analysis were fixed in a committed freeze file before the data were scored; *confirmatory* = a statement fixed in advance whose outcome is reported whatever it is; *exploratory* = everything else (including the features having been selected on dev text and evaluated on devtest, which was already examined in earlier work).

## 1. Setting in one paragraph

Gemma-2-2B, the layer-12 residual, the Gemma Scope 16,384-feature SAE (`blocks.12.hook_resid_post`); weights quantized by round-to-nearest per output channel at 16, 12, 8, 7, 6, 5 and 4 bits (INT0 is the unquantized identity control); 27 FLORES-200 languages; the language-selective feature groups of earlier work (K = 64 per language) with activity-matched control features; the `mask` regime (a BOS token that is never a statistic). The study asks whether the features selected as language-selective in full precision carry function for their language (F1), whether that survives quantization (F2), where the damage sits (F3), whether the language-association status used by earlier work predicts function (F4), whether the same feature does the same thing after quantization (F5), and, as the only confirmatory family, whether the groups matter for a downstream task (Belebele).

### What F5 says about the survival metric (the user's question of 2026-10-23)

(1) The activation-based survival criterion (token correlation with full precision at least 0.9) is only partly informative about function: at INT5, 82 % of the features that survive it are within noise against 46 % of those that do not, but about 31 % of the features whose function is intact are counted as lost; at INT4 it keeps 13 % while function is changed for every feature and identity is kept for 99.7 %. The write side stays closer to full precision than the read side at every precision (function is preserved better than activations). (2) The language-association status of the earlier study is mostly a margin statistic (A2, D-066: how borderline the feature already was predicts its loss, AUROC 0.90 at INT6), the margin is almost unrelated to function (A3, D-067: AUROC 0.58 for the function event at INT6), and the status carries no information about functional continuity at equal activity (descriptive AUROC of the status for the cosine 0.506 [0.469, 0.543]; retained-minus-lost differences of -0.001, -0.001 and +0.003 at INT6, INT5, INT4, all inconclusive). (3) The first of the three pre-proposed statements is met only because at INT6 almost every feature is within noise (90.2 % of the retained and 87.4 % of the lost ones): it does not discriminate. (4) The third is met (82.4 %), the second cannot be evaluated because at INT4 almost no feature stays within noise. (5) **The INT4 loss of similarity is not feature-specific:** ablating the decoder direction of a matched different feature at the same positions and amplitudes gives the same cosine between full precision and INT4 (0.828 against 0.842; D-061), so it reflects how the downstream computation and the activation amplitudes change under quantization for any SAE-direction perturbation; the features stay distinguishable and themselves. Taking the activations' own change out (the full-precision amplitude subtracted in every state) moves the feature's INT4 cosine only from 0.842 to 0.846 and the control's is 0.838: the drift sits in the quantized downstream map, not in the activations (D-063). The swap control at INT8, INT6 and INT5 (D-064) gives the same picture at every precision: the control's median cosine is 0.004, 0.006 and 0.007 below the features' (0.003 at the numerical floor), and 94, 90 and 66 % of the control's fingerprints are within the control's own floor (features in the same 404 features: 94, 92, 70 %). So the within-noise shares of point (3) are properties of the model's numerical drift shared by arbitrary directions, not a feature-specific preservation, and nowhere on the ladder is there a sign of feature-specific damage; feature-level functional continuity cannot be told apart from the model's response to any direction.

**Cannot be claimed:** that the groups matter for a downstream comprehension task (the frozen Belebele statement is not met); anything confirmatory beyond Belebele; that the results hold for other models, layers, quantizers or activation quantization (one model, one layer, one SAE, round-to-nearest weight quantization; Llama-3.2-1B is planned as a supporting model and has not been run); that the change of a feature's effect between full precision and a quantized model is specific to the feature at any precision from INT8 to INT4 (the swapped-direction control changes about as much; the features keep a small advantage of 0.003 to 0.014 in median cosine); that a language-selective feature is the only or a sufficient carrier of the language's ability (zero ablation tests necessity of the term, not sufficiency); that the shares "within noise" are properties of the features rather than of the strict floor (the floor is the 5th percentile of INT16, so the share cannot exceed about 0.95 even for noise).
