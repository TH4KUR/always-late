## RQ1. How do language selectivity and resource tier relate to activation preservation under different weight quantizers?

**Answer.** Preservation declines with precision, but selective-feature vulnerability and resource-tier disparities depend on the quantizer and feature activity. In the original BOS-masked RTN study none of the three initial contrasts rejects after Holm correction; the INT6 selective-minus-control survival difference is −1.44 percentage points (adjusted p = 0.128). This is not an equivalence test. The matched-RTN rerun changes the significance label of a small selective contrast, while GPTQ substantially improves survival. At INT4, GPTQ exceeds legacy RTN by 17.83 percentage points [16.29, 19.38] in the mean of 27 paired per-language survival differences; GPTQ exceeds matched RTN by 11.86 [11.18, 12.50].

The low/mid/high-tier INT6 survival percentages under legacy RTN are 69.4/90.6/95.1. Adjustment for activation scale attenuates the initial tier association. The later threshold-normalized perturbation study finds a tier pattern even in the INT16 rounding reference and an adjustment-sensitive GPTQ pattern. These observations support **quantizer- and scale-dependent multilingual preservation**, without identifying selective circuit destruction as the cause of downstream language disparities or showing that language disparities are absent.

## RQ2. What do interventions on language-selective feature groups change, and how does that depend on task and precision?

**Answer.** Removing the selected K64 group increases own-language next-token loss beyond activity-matched controls in all 27 languages. The Gemma-2-2B FP median excess harm is 0.262 nats/token. The relative intervention effect stays near its FP value at INT6 and rises at INT4: the median per-language retention ratio is about 1.54 under legacy RTN and 1.22 under GPTQ. These are intervention-effect ratios, not measures of improved language ability. Graded suppression supplies converging evidence of dependence. The original Study G repair rules are not met, while Extension C on 562 fresh sentences detects a tiny legacy-RTN benefit at ×1.1: −0.00135 nats/token [−0.0020, −0.0003] (97.5% interval; 20/27 languages improve, with Holm correction across the two primary ratios). GPTQ does not reproduce the fine-gain trend on the 300-sentence main-study sample; the 562-sentence extension was not repeated with GPTQ. The data support a small recipe-dependent improvement, rather than substantial repair.

The reviewed Gemma-2-9B original-RTN extension shows the same broad precision pattern: FP harm is positive in all 27 languages, and INT4 median retention is 1.586 [1.238, 2.264] (language bootstrap). Its macro mean of ratios is 2.479 [1.781, 3.368], while its ratio of mean effects is 1.474 [1.263, 1.937]. All paired 2B–9B differences reported in the review include zero; the extension supports consistency across these two sizes without establishing a size effect. A sign error and a tautological criterion in the original 9B prediction list are documented, so it is not described as five independent confirmations. The Llama check does not satisfy its separate frozen consistency rule.

On held-out Belebele, the selected group's FP excess correct-option loss is −0.0001 nats/item [−0.0035, +0.0031]; the required positive FP effect is not established. Accuracy is a separate endpoint (unedited about 42.6%). Positive controls produce measurable effects, so this is an informative task-specific null at the design's sensitivity, not evidence that the selected features never matter for comprehension. The combined result is **causal relevance for the tested text-prediction edits with task-dependent behavioral relevance**. It does not establish exclusive language circuits.

## RQ3. How informative are activation survival and language-association retention about preservation of an intervention's effect?

**Answer.** They measure different properties and provide complementary information. At legacy INT4, only 13.2% of the 1,487-feature F5 sample exceeds activation correlation 0.9, yet the median effect-fingerprint cosine is 0.839 [0.829, 0.848] and own-feature identity retrieval is 99.7%. Effect drift therefore does not imply that features have lost all functional identity. The binary association-retention label does not usefully discriminate effect preservation in the tested comparisons; continuous activation correlation is more informative.

On the 1,412 reliable-feature population, adding activation correlation to activity/effect-size baselines reduces language-held-out prediction MAE by 3.1–4.4% across INT6/INT4 and all three 2B quantizers, with every 95% language-bootstrap interval above zero. This is a **small positive predictive contribution**. It falls below the project's 10% practical convention, which is not a boundary between truth and no information. Adding gain and threshold-normalized encoder perturbation on top gives estimates −0.4% to +1.8%, with all six intervals including zero.

The streamed measurement audit reproduces prior correlations within 3e−8, checks the exact gain/noise identity to about 1e−14, and separates gain, noise, firing flips and threshold-relative perturbations over 214,506 active language–feature cells. These diagnostics improve interpretation of activation changes without demonstrating additional prediction benefit in the tested setting. They do not establish that correlation is universally useless or that SAEs are unreliable.

Sources: [`functional_validation/results/f5/report.md`](functional_validation/results/f5/report.md), [`survival_effect_rescue/stage4_gemma_report_2026-10-28.md`](survival_effect_rescue/stage4_gemma_report_2026-10-28.md), [`survival_measurement/stage3/stage3_results.md`](survival_measurement/stage3/stage3_results.md).

## RQ4. How do context, downstream weights and generic sensitivity affect transfer of a fixed intervention across precision states?

**Answer.** The crossed-prefix/suffix experiment separates context, downstream-weight and interaction contributions to a fixed FP-amplitude edit. The terms add exactly as vectors. Their norms, medians and loss projections need not add. For legacy INT4, group net harm is 0.848 versus 0.263 at FP, with median absolute context/weight terms 0.310/0.104 nats/token. Using the quantized model's own amplitude reduces group harm to 0.433. At the feature-vector level the downstream-weight term is larger, so the attribution depends on the intervention and endpoint.

At INT4 the median feature-level transfer cosine is 0.768/0.822/0.877 and normalized error 0.664/0.572/0.476 for legacy RTN/matched RTN/GPTQ. Thus GPTQ preserves intervention effects more closely under the matched experimental recipe. Similar control-direction drift identifies a substantial generic sensitivity component. On the main panel, generic sensitivity improves held-out transfer-error prediction by 21.5% [12.7, 29.4] at legacy INT4; its INT6 interval includes zero. The original combined-width utility criterion is not met. On fresh Wikipedia text, the criterion is met at both widths for the RTN arms, while GPTQ meets it at INT4 but not INT6. Averaging fingerprints improves apparent agreement relative to context-level agreement.

These are **measured boundaries of intervention portability**, not evidence that all interpretability findings fail to transfer. The external text check supports the INT4 pattern; Wikipedia-domain GPTQ calibration and numerical floors qualify the comparisons. Calibration and error compensation are jointly changed by GPTQ, so the experiment does not isolate their separate causes.

## What kind of contribution this supports

The contribution is a controlled empirical evaluation of multilingual feature and intervention preservation, with an exact measurement decomposition and multiple functional controls. Its value rests on the contrasts, precision controls, numerical checks and convergence of several outcomes. The fixed dictionary makes the paired question well defined. Model, layer and dictionary coverage bound external validity; they do not erase the observed contrasts. SAE nonuniqueness, incomplete disentanglement and task-dependent interpretation are shared methodological questions in the field and are addressed here through operational definitions and intervention controls rather than claims of a complete circuit account.

Most analyses are exploratory or post hoc; the reported frozen-stage choices and intervals improve transparency without turning reused data into independent confirmation. The only fully confirmatory family is the original held-out Belebele family. The work supports a complete empirical/measurement manuscript; no acceptance or exhaustive novelty claim is made.
