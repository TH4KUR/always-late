# Stage 3 results in plain words (D-124)

Written 2026-10-29 by Claude from [`stage3_results.md`](stage3_results.md) (generated by `scripts/g0/survival_stage3.py`; the numbers below are all in that file or in `stage3_results.json`). Rules: [`../analysis_freeze.json`](../analysis_freeze.json) (D-120, committed before any Stage 2 output existed), D-123 (what the review of the generator added before the run) and D-121 (the user's reporting rule: **every improvement is reported with its interval, the thresholds 10 %, 0.2 / 0.5 and 0.20 are conventions and not claims, nulls and inconclusive outcomes are results**). Post hoc relative to Stage 1 (same tokens and features were seen as correlations). One model (Gemma-2-2B), one layer, one SAE, three simulated weight-only quantizers, 27 languages.

**What was measured.** For each of 214,506 (language, feature) cells that are active in full precision (27,036 equal-budget tokens per language, mask regime), a sidecar run streamed the sums behind the survival correlation r: the gain g (how much of the full-precision activation is kept), the residual noise, how often the feature switches on or off, and nu = the typical size of the change of the encoder's pre-activation in units of the feature's own threshold. Three quantizers: legacy RTN, matched RTN, GPTQ; INT6 and INT4 (and INT16 for legacy RTN as a near-zero-change reference).

**Checks that must hold before any number counts (all held).** The sidecar reproduces the committed Phase 1 survival correlations to 3e-8 (float32 resolution) with identical counts of surviving features in every language and arm (G2(ii)); the full-precision side is bitwise identical across the three jobs (0 cells differ, D-123); the identity "r from gain, spread and residual equals r from the sums" holds to 1e-14 on 214,267 to 214,502 cells per condition (4 to 239 degenerate cells per condition are excluded and counted: no residual variance, a clamped correlation or a silent feature).

## 1. What one quantizer does to a typical feature (descriptive medians over cells)

| | nu (share of the threshold) | gain g | active tokens switched off | median lambda |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| legacy INT16 | 0.018 | 0.997 | 1.1 % | -4.78 |
| legacy INT6 / matched / GPTQ | 0.130 / 0.113 / 0.086 | 0.958 / 0.967 / 0.976 | 9.2 / 7.7 / 5.9 % | -2.45 / -2.61 / -2.96 |
| legacy INT4 / matched / GPTQ | 0.706 / 0.562 / 0.378 | 0.594 / 0.672 / 0.823 | 50.0 / 42.9 / 27.0 % | -0.03 / -0.46 / -1.00 |

At INT4 under legacy RTN the median feature loses half of its active tokens and its activations shrink to about 60 %; the survival correlation r is blind to the gain, so its "survival" says little about this. GPTQ's lower noise-to-signal ratio lambda at INT4 (median -0.98 against legacy) comes mostly from keeping the gain closer to 1 (median of the gain term -0.62, of the noise term -0.27; the medians of the parts do not add up to the median of the whole), at INT6 mostly from lower noise (-0.46 against -0.04). The predicted share of flips (perturbation larger than the distance to the threshold) tracks the exact share with Spearman 0.71 to 0.96. Between quantizers log nu is strongly but not perfectly related across features (Spearman 0.81 to 0.83 matched against legacy, 0.66 to 0.71 GPTQ against legacy); GPTQ's median log nu is 0.41 (INT6) and 0.61 (INT4) lower than legacy's, matched RTN's 0.14 and 0.22 lower.

## 2. H3: does lambda follow the feature's size, and nu not? (convention: |rho| > 0.5 and < 0.2)

Per language, Spearman correlation with the log of the feature's full-precision spread (sigma_a), median over 27 languages: lambda -0.51 to -0.53, nu -0.004 to 0.114 (six cells). **The convention is met in all six cells, but the lambda half only just (smallest value -0.507, legacy INT4).** Sensitivity with the peak activation as the scale (the Stage 1 proxy): lambda -0.72 to -0.75, but nu 0.25 to 0.41: **nu is nearly free of a feature's spread but not of its peak size.** So "metric 2 removes the feature scale" holds for the spread (the quantity in lambda's formula) and not for the peak.

## 3. H4: is there a tier pattern in how hard the quantizer pushes? (outcome per language: minus the mean of log nu; higher tier = less perturbation)

In plain numbers: the median nu by tier (low / mid / high) is 0.0192 / 0.0179 / 0.0170 at legacy INT16, 0.146 / 0.132 / 0.123 at INT6 and 0.80 / 0.72 / 0.655 at INT4: low-resource languages' features are pushed 13 to 22 % harder than high-resource ones, **and already at INT16**, where the weights barely change. The tier slope of -mean log nu (per tier step, script families as nuisance) is 0.063 at INT16 and 0.077 / 0.084 (legacy INT6 / INT4), 0.077 / 0.085 (matched): a change of 0.014 to 0.022 per step, i.e. 1 to 2 % of nu.
- **Pre-specified primary (the quantization-specific part, standardized per width; "absent" needs the 90 % interval inside +-0.20):** legacy INT6 absent (s = -0.07), legacy INT4 inconclusive (-0.10, 90 % interval -0.203 to -0.023, just outside the bound), matched INT6 absent (-0.02), matched INT4 absent (-0.06); **GPTQ present at both widths (s = -0.79 and -0.90, 95 % intervals -1.34 to -0.35 and -1.39 to -0.42): under GPTQ the tier gradient of nu almost vanishes** (slope 0.012 and 0.002; median nu by tier at INT4 0.372 / 0.389 / 0.370).
- **But this is fragile.** With the language's median spread as a covariate the GPTQ cells reverse (s = +0.23, inconclusive at both widths; slope 0.075 / 0.089 against 0.038 at INT16), and the sensitivity with the INT16 spread as common denominator calls legacy INT6 / INT4 and matched INT4 "present" (0.21 / 0.30 / 0.32) because the across-language spread of log nu at INT16 is small. What can be said without relying on one standardization: for both RTN variants the tier gradient of the perturbation is about the same at INT16, INT6 and INT4 (a property of the features and languages, not specific to quantization); for GPTQ it disappears or reverses depending on how the feature scale is handled, which this study cannot settle.
- Secondary (the tier pattern itself, standardized slope against 0): present for legacy and matched RTN (0.81 to 0.89; 0.91 at INT16), 0.41 to 0.54 with the covariate (matched INT4 inconclusive); inconclusive for GPTQ without the covariate (0.12 and 0.015), present with it (0.78).
- "Inconclusive" is an accepted result: about 23 effective languages (four script families hold one language each).

Reading: adding the activation correlation R1 to the baselines gives a small improvement in all six cells (3 to 4 %, intervals above zero). **Adding the gain and nu on top of R1 gives no improvement distinguishable from zero in any of the six cells** (point estimates -0.4 % to +1.8 %, every interval includes zero; the INT4 cells lean slightly positive, the INT6 cells slightly negative; the one interval entirely below zero, g only, GPTQ INT6, is 0.1 % wide). None reaches the 10 % convention (the D-113 label "no clear predictive value beyond baselines" is that convention); the numbers above are the result.

## 5. Selectivity (secondary, descriptive)

Language-selective cells (the feature is selective and peaks in that language) have a lower nu than cells of features that are selective for no language: mean difference in log nu -0.15 to -0.22 (95 % intervals about +-0.05, all 27 languages, all six cells), i.e. selective features are pushed about 14 to 19 % less hard. No adjustment for feature size was made.

## 6. What this says about the paper's frame, and what it does not

- Reported survival rates depend on the quantizer and on feature size, and a fixed cut-off mixes both: supported (lambda follows the spread at -0.5 and the peak at -0.7; the quantizers differ by shifts in noise and gain that r does not separate).
- Metric 1 shows why; metric 2 removes the spread but not the peak: supported for the first half, partly for the second.
- Activation agreement of this kind carries limited information about preserved effects: R1 gives a small improvement (3 to 4 %); gain and nu add nothing distinguishable from zero.
- The tier pattern in how hard the quantizer pushes exists at INT16 and is nearly the same at INT6 and INT4 for both RTN variants: it is not a quantization-specific language effect. For GPTQ the picture depends on the scale adjustment and is not settled here.
- Not shown: any deployment, kernel or activation-quantization claim; any other model, layer, SAE or quantizer; why GPTQ differs.
