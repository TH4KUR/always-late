## 1. What is tested, and what is not

"Minimum" = the findings of the Gemma study that carry the paper, each as a directional, pre-written check on a second model with a second SAE family, using the SAME analysis code on item files of the SAME format:

- **L1 (Gemma claim 1).** At full precision, zeroing a language's 64 most selective SAE features harms that language's next-token loss beyond activity-matched control features.
- **L2 (Gemma claim 2).** That dependence is preserved under round-to-nearest weight quantization through the middle of the ladder and changes at the deep end (Gemma: macro retention 1.01 at INT8, 1.04 at INT6, 1.13 at INT5, 2.14 at INT4).
- **L3 (Study G, H-G1 and the INT4 threshold).** Turning the group down by 0.354, 0.177 and 0.088 harms the loss in a graded way (more for the deeper turn-down), consistently across languages, and the deepest turn-down costs more at INT4 than at full precision (Gemma: 1.9 times).

Not tested here (stated so that nobody reads more into the result): the INT4 repair at ratios 1.1 and 1.2 (needs 562 reserve sentences and five control draws, D-082), the Belebele task (the model is small and the languages are not all covered; the frozen task statement stays a Gemma statement), F5 (single-feature fingerprints), the state-defined groups, the optimal-gain analysis. A positive result says "the same qualitative picture on a second model and SAE family"; it does not make the numbers equal and does not turn any Gemma claim from exploratory to confirmatory.

## 2. Setting (everything that is new is listed; everything else is the frozen Gemma code)

- **Model:** `meta-llama/Llama-3.2-1B` (16 layers, d_model 2048), bf16, TransformerLens `from_pretrained_no_processing` (the call of their function `load_gemma_tl_model` with another name), BOS prepended and excluded from every statistic (mask regime, as for Gemma).
- **SAE (primary):** `seonglae/Llama-3.2-1B-sae`, folder `Llama-3.2-1B_blocks.12.hook_resid_pre_14336_topk_48_0.0002_42_fineweb_512` (TopK, k = 48, 14,336 features, trained on FineWeb: the web-text analogue of Gemma Scope; seed 42), hook `blocks.12.hook_resid_pre`, `apply_b_dec_to_input` true, no activation normalisation (cfg read on Ada, 2026-10-26). Depth 12 of 16 (0.75; Gemma: 12 of 26, 0.46): a difference of the setting, stated with every result. **Sensitivity SAE (run only if the primary run is complete and the GPU time allows, same predictions):** `..._42_faithful-llama3.2-1b_512` (FaithfulSAE, trained on the model's own generated text, no external dataset; Cho et al. found that SAE families differ in how causally anchored their features are).
- **Hook:** the residual `blocks.12.hook_resid_pre` is the tensor `blocks.11.hook_resid_post`; the frozen code uses `LAYER` with `hook_resid_post`, `stop_at_layer = LAYER + 1` and `start_at_layer = LAYER + 1`, so the adapter sets `LAYER = 11` and tells the loaded SAE that its hook is `blocks.11.hook_resid_post`; the equality of the two ways to read the residual is a test on a tiny random Llama and a gate on the real model.
- **Quantizer:** their `quantize_rtn_per_output_channel_tl`, unchanged, on the TransformerLens tensors (`W_Q`, `W_K`, `W_V`, `W_O`, `W_in`, `W_gate`, `W_out`; for the grouped-query attention of Llama the keys and values are the `_W_K` and `_W_V` tensors, which the same name rule matches); embeddings, norms and the unembedding stay in bf16. States: FP, INT8, INT6, INT5, INT4 (INT7 and the rounding-only controls are left out: minimum).
- **Languages and text:** the same 27 FLORES-200 languages and the same sentence ids as for Gemma: selection on dev (997 sentences, equal token budget per language), evaluation on the 300 held-out devtest sentences `lm_ids(1012, 150, 300, 0)['eval']`, own-language text.
- **Language-selective groups and controls:** the frozen Phase 1 selectivity procedure on the Llama SAE (`phase1_run.run_tagging` and `selectivity_report`: support rule, bootstrap stability of the peak language at 0.8 of 100 draws, S above the shuffled null cutoff), then the frozen `phase1_func_groups.build_manifest` (K16, K64, all; dev mean-activation ranking; activity-matched controls from the supported non-selective pool; seed 0). A language with fewer than 16 selective features is NOT tested (reported as such); a group with fewer than 64 members is the whole selective set (K64 = min(64, n)); the proportional counts below use the number n of languages that are tested.
- **Edit and metric:** the frozen edit path (SAE activations of the set scaled, SAE error kept), harm = edited minus unedited next-token loss in the same state, E = group harm minus the mean harm of three matched-control draws, paired by sentence; the frozen runner `phase1_func_run.py` and the Study G preset `gain|0.3535533905932738,0.1767766952966369,0.08838834764831845|c3|K64` (zero-ablation of the group and of three controls, the three down ratios of the group and of the controls, the sham), through the wrapper `phase1_gain_run.py`; the ratios are the Gemma ones (no new calibration: no new freedom).
- **Analysis:** the frozen `phase1_func_analysis.py` (E and retention) and the Study G analysis rules `phase1_gain_analysis.py` restricted to the down ratios (a restriction written with its test before the run).
- **New code (tests first, mutation check):** `phase1_llama_sae.py` (TopK SAE loader: cfg checks, encode, decode), `phase1_llama_run.py` (the adapter: sets the constants, injects the SAE loader, then calls the frozen tagging runner, the manifest builder or the frozen T-LM runner), a Llama comparison report module. No frozen file is edited.

## 3. Predictions (written now; thresholds from the Gemma numbers; each is met or not met, and the report says which)

Let n be the number of tested languages (at least 16 selective features) and `req(x) = ceil(x * n / 27)` (the proportional rule of Study G).

- **P1 (L1):** at FP the median over the tested languages of E(K64 zero-ablation) is above 0 with its 95 % bootstrap interval (over languages) above 0, and E > 0 in at least `req(24)` languages (Gemma: 27 of 27, macro 0.70).
- **P2a (L2, flat through the middle):** macro retention (E at the state over E at FP) lies within [0.85, 1.15] at INT8 and INT6.
- **P2b (L2, the deep end changes):** macro retention at INT4 is at least 0.30 above the retention at INT6 and is the largest of the ladder (Gemma: 2.14 against 1.04). INT5 is reported, not tested (Gemma 1.13: a gradual rise is not excluded).
- **P3a (L3, graded):** at FP and at INT4 the median net harm over languages is strictly decreasing in the ratio (0 > 0.088 > 0.177 > 0.354 in harm order) and the order holds in at least `req(24)` languages (H-G1 of Study G).
- **P3b (L3, the threshold):** the net harm at the deepest down ratio (0.088) is larger at INT4 than at FP in at least `req(20)` languages and the median ratio INT4 over FP is above 1.3 (Gemma: 1.9).

**Verdict wording (fixed now):** *consistent* if P1, P2b and P3a hold (the qualitative picture: a language-specific, graded dependence that is flat through INT6 and larger at INT4); *partly consistent* if P1 and P3a hold and P2b or P3b does not (the dependence exists but the quantization pattern differs: the numbers are reported with the reason candidates, not explained away); *not consistent* if P1 fails. P2a and P3b are reported with their outcomes and do not change the verdict. A different pattern on Llama is a result about Llama, not a failure of the pipeline, provided the gates pass and the planted-world tests hold.

## 5. Costs and risks

GPU: tagging about 20 minutes, the T-LM job about 0.5 to 0.8 hour per state (459 configurations per state against 999 for Gemma, a model about two and a half times cheaper per pass): about 3 to 4 hours for five states on one slot, half of that on two. Risks: (a) few selective features in low-resource languages for this SAE (handled by the 16-feature floor, reported); (b) the frozen runner has an unseen Gemma assumption (the smoke job and the gates exist to find it; a needed change is a new adapter function, never an edit of a frozen file); (c) the quantization pattern of a 1B model may differ for reasons unrelated to the question (reported as a difference, with the setting differences listed: depth, width, SAE family, TopK against JumpReLU, tokenizer); (d) the Llama licence: the weights stay on Ada's cache, are not copied into the repository, and nothing derived from them that reproduces the weights is published.

## 8. Interim observation and a post hoc robustness plan (05:52, 2026-10-26; written BEFORE the comparison module was run and before the other states exist; nothing in sections 3, 6 and 7 is changed)

**What was seen, and when.** The FP state of `llama-lm-a` finished (`func_lm_eval_FP.pt`, sha256 `458f5170...`, equal to Ada's after `scp`) and was read with the frozen functional-effect function, language by language, to see whether the pipeline produces sensible numbers, before any prediction was evaluated by the comparison module. Facts: E (zero-ablation of the 64-feature group minus the mean of three matched-control draws) is positive in 21 of the 27 languages, the median E is +0.048 nats per token (Gemma: +0.26), and E is strongly positive in some languages (deu 1.16, yor 1.50, amh 1.21, vie 0.30, spa 0.27, por 0.24, pol 0.23) and negative in others (jpn -1.23, kor -0.59, rus -0.08, hin -0.09, zho -0.01, kan -0.00). **Consequence for P1: the count rule (at least req(24) = 24 of 27) is not met by the FP data (21 of 27); the comparison module, which uses the same function, will give the same count, so the verdict by the pre-registered function is 'not consistent'. This is recorded now so that no later step can pretend to have been blind to it.**

**An observation that qualifies how the negative languages are to be read (a diagnostic, not a change of a prediction).** The per-draw control harms of the negative languages are heavy-tailed: jpn controls 0.07, 0.20 and 3.72 nats (the group 0.095), kor controls 1.85, 0.04 and 0.05 (the group 0.051): one control draw of three contains a set of non-selective features whose removal doubles the loss, and the mean of three draws is dominated by it. The pool of controls on this SAE is small (898 features, Gemma 2,860) and holds features that are not language-neutral in their effect. The matching (language's own dev mean activation and firing rate) does not prevent that.

**Post hoc analyses planned (labelled post hoc in every table; they cannot change the verdict of section 7; the code gets tests first and is committed before it is run on the Llama files):** (R1) E with the MEDIAN of the three control draws instead of their mean; (R2) per language, whether the group harm exceeds every single control draw, and the number of languages with this property; (R3) a flag for languages in which one control draw has a harm above five times the median of the other two (an outlier draw) and the count of positive languages without them; (R4) if the GPU time allows after the main jobs, the two further control draws of the manifest (draws 3 and 4 of the five drawn for K64) at FP and INT4 for all languages, which gives E with five draws; (R5) the same R1 to R3 at INT4. A result of the robustness analyses that differs from the pre-registered one is reported next to it, never in its place.
