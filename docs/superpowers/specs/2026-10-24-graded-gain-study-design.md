# Study G: graded gain of language-selective features across the precision ladder (design)

Written 2026-10-24, before any number of this study exists. Request of the user (verbatim, with the status of the study): [`reports/functional_validation/gain_study/README.md`](../../../reports/functional_validation/gain_study/README.md). Plan: [`../plans/2026-10-24-graded-gain-study.md`](../plans/2026-10-24-graded-gain-study.md). Builds on the functional-validation design ([`2026-10-21-functional-validation-design.md`](2026-10-21-functional-validation-design.md)), whose instrument it reuses unchanged.

## 1. Question

The earlier functional study removes a language's selective features entirely (scale 0) and finds that the next-token loss on that language rises, in all 27 languages, and more so in quantized models (D-040 onward). It does not say **how the loss depends on how strongly the features are driven**. Study G measures that dependence:

For each of the 27 languages, at full precision (FP) and at INT8, INT7, INT6, INT5, INT4 (RTN, per-output-channel weights), multiply the activations of that language's selective features by three ratios below 1 ("turn down") and three ratios above 1 ("turn up"), and measure the next-token loss on that language's own text. Then ask:

- **Q1 (dose, down)**: does the loss of the language rise monotonically as its features are turned down further?
- **Q2 (dose, up)**: does it change monotonically as they are turned up, and in which direction?
- **Q3 (precision)**: for the same ratio, does the effect grow monotonically as the weights are quantized more coarsely?
- **Q4 (specificity)**: is each effect larger than that of the same ratio applied to matched control features that are not selective for the language?
- **Q5 (consistency)**: do the languages agree in sign, order and shape, given that the sets of selective features of different languages are different?
- **Q6 (baseline)**: before any intervention, which features contribute to each language and which are general, in every precision state, and how do the group's activations change with quantization?

Q6 is the baseline the user asked for ("first measure baseline before and after each quantization"); Q1 to Q5 are the intervention sweeps.

## 2. Definitions (all fixed here, none depends on a result)

- **State** s: FP, INT8, INT7, INT6, INT5, INT4: the layer-12 Gemma Scope 16k SAE of Gemma-2-2B (hook `blocks.12.hook_resid_post`), weights quantized by RTN per output channel at the stated width, activations and SAE in full precision (as in every earlier job). BOS masked (the mask regime).
- **Group G_l**: the frozen `K64` group of language l from `reports/functional_validation/groups_manifest.json` (sha256 of its source `2f0422c2…`): the 64 features that were selective for l on FLORES **dev** at full precision and have the largest dev mean activation on l. The group is the same in every state (it is defined in FP and carried through the ladder; this is the question "does the same set of features still matter"). Size `all` (73 to 1,719 features) is a secondary size (section 8).
- **Matched controls C_l,r** (r = 1..3): the first three of the manifest's five `K64` control draws for l: 64 supported non-selective features matched one-to-one to the group members on dev mean activation and firing rate on l (greedy nearest neighbour; `phase1_func_groups.draw_controls`). They carry the claim of specificity: scaling any 64 features of similar activity changes the loss somewhat, and the group effect is measured beyond that.
- **Edit with ratio α** (`phase1_func_intervene.apply_edit`, kind `scale`): at every valid (non-BOS, non-pad) position of the sentence, the SAE activations `a` of the features in the set are replaced by `α·a`, and the residual is changed by `Σ (α−1)·a_f·W_dec[f]` over the set; the SAE reconstruction error term is kept untouched. α = 1 is the identity (a gate of the runner proves it bitwise), α = 0 is zero-ablation. The edit acts only where the feature fires (a position where `a_f = 0` is not changed): this is **not** steering by adding a direction, it is a gain change.
- **Text**: the 300 held-out FLORES devtest sentences per language of the frozen evaluation (`lm_ids(1012, 150, 300, 0)["eval"]`, the same sentence ids in all languages, as in every earlier evaluation job), and the 150 pilot sentences for calibration only. The metric is the mean next-token NLL (nats per token, token-weighted over the language's content positions), as in F1.
- **Loss** `L(l, s, set, α)`. **Baseline** `L0(l, s)` = unedited loss in the same state (the runner's `none` configuration, same batch pass). **Harm** `h = L(edited) − L0(l, s)` (nats per token; positive = worse), paired per sentence. **Net harm** `h_net(l, s, α) = h(G_l, α) − mean_r h(C_l,r, α)`. **Total change** `T = L(edited, s) − L0(l, FP)` (what a user of the quantized model sees compared with the original model).
- **Optimal gain** `α*(l, s)` = the ratio of the grid (including α = 1 and the six study ratios, not zero-ablation) with the lowest loss in state s on l; `α* > 1` means turning the group up improves the quantized model.

## 3. The ratios (a calibration on the pilot split, with a rule fixed before it runs)

The user asked for "a sensible amount" and three ratios each way. The pilot already holds two points (K64, 150 pilot sentences, median over the 27 languages, nats per token): scale 0.5: FP 0.0092, INT4 0.0496; scale 2: FP 0.0303, INT4 0.0211; zero-ablation: FP 0.2973, INT4 0.5182; zeroed matched control: FP 0.0218, INT4 0.0306. So halving is inside the control noise at FP while zero-ablation is 13 times larger: the response is strongly non-linear and the informative down ratios lie closer to 0 than a fixed ladder would put them. Therefore the ratios are chosen by a rule from a pilot calibration, never from the evaluation sentences:

1. **Grid** (log-symmetric): down `2^(-k/2)`, k = 1..8 (0.707, 0.5, 0.354, 0.25, 0.177, 0.125, 0.088, 0.0625); up the reciprocals (1.41, 2, 2.83, 4, 5.66, 8, 11.3, 16). Calibration jobs: FP and INT4, pilot split (150 sentences per language), groups K64 and two control draws per language scaled at every grid ratio, plus zero-ablation (α = 0) and the unedited baseline.
2. **Rule.** Let `z` be the FP median (over the 27 languages) net harm of zero-ablation (group minus mean control, both zeroed). **Down ratios**: for each target fraction φ in {0.10, 0.30, 0.60}, the grid value whose FP median net harm is nearest to φ·z (absolute difference of net harm); if two targets pick the same value, the next nearest not yet chosen is taken for the later one; if the median net harm of the grid never exceeds 0.10·z (the response is flat down to 0.0625), the three down ratios are 0.25, 0.125, 0.0625 and the study says so. **Up ratios**: the reciprocals of the three chosen down ratios (symmetric in log gain, so that "×k up" mirrors "÷k down").
3. The chosen six ratios, the table they came from and the date are appended to the decision log **before** the main jobs are submitted. Zero-ablation (α = 0) is kept as the endpoint of the down axis in every main job and α = 1 (unedited) as its origin; they are reported but are not counted among the three ratios.

## 4. Runs (Ada, RTX 2080 Ti; at most two jobs at a time; the frozen runner is wrapped, never edited)

- Entry point: a wrapper module `scripts/g0/phase1_gain_run.py` that registers a preset in the frozen runner's `PRESETS` at run time and wraps `build_configs` to add the scaled controls; `scripts/g0/phase1_func_run.py` stays byte-identical (hash-checked by `test_phase1_func_freeze_mc`).
- Per language and state the main job runs: unedited `none`; `zero_K64` and `ctrl{0,1,2}_K64` (zero-ablation of the group and of the three controls, the endpoint); `scale{α}_K64` for the six ratios; `gscale{α}_ctrl{r}_K64` for the six ratios on each of the three control draws; `sham_K64` (an exactness check): 1 + 1 + 3 + 6 + 18 + 1 = **30** configurations on the language's own text only (no off-target panel). Estimated 1.5 to 2 GPU hours per state, 6 states: 9 to 12 GPU hours, about 5 to 6 hours of wall time on two slots (to be measured by the calibration jobs).
- Split by state: one job FP, INT8, INT7; another INT6, INT5, INT4 (the runner saves every state when it finishes and does not start one that the time limit would cut).

## 5. Pre-registered hypotheses and decision rules

Everything is **exploratory** in status (the FLORES devtest sentences were examined in earlier studies, D-039); the rules below fix in advance what counts as support, so that the reading cannot bend to the result. "Languages" counts out of 27; a language "agrees" when its point estimate satisfies the statement; confidence intervals are paired bootstraps over sentences (2,000 draws, seed 0).

- **H-G1 (dose, down).** At FP, net harm increases strictly with depth of turn-down, `h_net(d1) < h_net(d2) < h_net(d3) < h_net(0)` for the three chosen down ratios d1 > d2 > d3 and zero-ablation. Supported if at least 24 of 27 languages agree **and** the median over languages is strictly ordered. Reported at every state, with Spearman ρ of (depth, net harm) per language.
- **H-G2 (down is specific).** At the deepest chosen down ratio, the lower bound of the 95 % interval of `h_net` is above 0 in at least 24 of 27 languages (FP). The mildest ratio is reported without a threshold (expected inside the control noise).
- **H-G3 (dose, up).** At FP, harm of the group is strictly increasing in the up ratio (`h(u1) < h(u2) < h(u3)`, u1 < u2 < u3) in at least 24 of 27 languages; and the group harm exceeds the matched-control harm at u3 (lower bound of `h_net` > 0) in at least 20 of 27. If the curve instead decreases or is not monotone, that is the result.
- **H-G4 (precision, down).** For every chosen down ratio and every language, harm is non-decreasing as the state goes FP → INT8 → INT7 → INT6 → INT5 → INT4 (Kendall τ of (bits removed, harm) per language). Supported if, at the deepest down ratio, τ > 0 in at least 24 of 27 languages and the median over languages is non-decreasing along the ladder; INT8 and INT7 are expected to equal FP within noise (the earlier ladder is flat to INT8), so ties of less than one bootstrap standard error are not counted as violations.
- **H-G5 (repair by turning up).** At INT4 the macro curve of harm against the up ratios dips below 0 for at least one mild up ratio (the quantized model gets better when the group is amplified); supported if the 95 % interval of the median over languages excludes 0 on the negative side at some up ratio and `α*(l, INT4) > 1` in at least 14 of 27 languages. Related, graded prediction from the baseline (Q6): `α*(l, s)` tracks the inverse of the group's activation change on the language, `g(l, s) = (mean activation of G_l on l in state s) / (same in FP)`: rank correlation across languages of `α*(l, INT4)` and `1/g(l, INT4)` above 0.3.
- **H-G6 (consistency across languages).** For each state and ratio the sign of `h_net` agrees in at least 24 of 27 languages for the deepest down ratio; the languages' rankings by net harm correlate between FP and INT4 (Spearman across languages) above 0.5; heterogeneity is reported by the spread (IQR) of per-language net harm divided by the language's zero-ablation net harm (a language-independent dose axis).
- **H-G7 (baseline).** Before any intervention: the share of each language's SAE activation mass carried by its own group and the retained fraction of the group's peak association decline with decreasing width in the same order as the D-035 association retention (INT8 > INT7 > INT6 > INT5 > INT4), in at least 24 of 27 languages for INT4 against FP.

Not hypotheses of this study, but reported: raw and per-token-normalised harms (`h/L0`), Belebele accuracy is not part of it (the earlier family was null at the group's size), and no p-values are corrected across cells: the cell tables are descriptive, the counts above are the decision rules.

## 6. Baseline measurement (G1, CPU, before any GPU job)

From the D-035 profiles `association-full/association/eq/profiles.pt` (per condition FP, INT8, INT7, INT6, INT5, INT4, plus floor conditions; per language token counts, positive counts, activation sums and maxima of all 16,384 features; FLORES devtest, equal-budget stream, 27,036 content tokens per language). For every state and language: the number of features whose unique firing-rate peak is still the language; the number of features selective for it at that state (supported by the Phase 1 rule, `S ≥` the FP null cutoff, unique peak = l) and their overlap with the FP-defined cohort and with `G_l`; the mean activation of `G_l` on l and its ratio `g(l, s)` to FP; the shares of the language's total activation mass carried by `G_l`, by the language's other selective features, by other languages' selective features, by the matched-control pool and by all other features ("general"). Because these profiles are computed on devtest, they **describe** each state but are never used to **select** a group that is evaluated on devtest. A properly state-defined group (selected on dev text in the quantized state) would need a new collection job on dev text; it is an optional extension (section 8).

## 7. Analysis (G5) and figures (G6)

Per language and state: the curve `h(α)` for the group, the mean control and the net harm, with intervals; monotonicity counts per rule above; `α*`; the cross-language consistency measures; macro (median over languages and mean of per-language values) curves by state. Figures: (1) the dose-response curves of FP in 27 small panels, (2) the macro curve per state on one axis (the main figure: how the dose-response shifts with quantization), (3) heatmap language × state at the deepest down ratio and at the largest up ratio, (4) α* by state with the baseline prediction, (5) specificity (group against control by ratio). Every figure is rendered and looked at before it is committed.

## 8. Limits and extensions

- Layer 12 only, one SAE (a fixed 16k basis), RTN only, one model; the loss is a language-model measure (the task-accuracy family was null at this group size, D-050, D-062, D-069). The group is FP-defined and small for most languages (median 192 selective features; K64 is the top 64): large effects of turning 64 features of 16,384 up or down are not expected, and a null at the mild ratios is a finding.
- Scaling a feature that fires rarely changes few positions; the activation-weighted size of the edit is reported (the runner records the perturbation norm ratio and the number of active group features).
- Amplification beyond the natural range of a feature is out of distribution for the model; the study reports the dependence on the ratio and does not claim that large up ratios are a repair method.
- Extensions after the main result, in this order, each its own appended decision: (a) size `all` at FP, INT6, INT4 with the same ratios (does the whole selective set behave like its top 64?); (b) state-defined groups on dev text (a collection job; D-035 collector adapted); (c) Belebele accuracy at the same ratios for the 18 covered languages; (d) a second seed of control draws if specificity is marginal.

## 9. Amendment A1 (2026-10-24 07:12, after the baseline G1 and before any intervention number; nothing above is changed)

The baseline (`reports/functional_validation/gain_study/baseline/report.md`, descriptive, from the D-035 devtest profiles) shows, in the median over the 27 languages: the activation of the group K64 on its own language is 1.000, 0.998, 0.995, 0.990, **0.852** of FP at INT8, INT7, INT6, INT5, INT4; the language's whole activation mass is 0.997, 1.000, 0.994, 1.015, **0.843** and the matched-control pool 0.998, 1.001, 0.992, 1.010, **0.853**. So the INT4 shrinkage of the layer-12 SAE activations is about 15 % **for every class of feature**, not for the group (group over whole-language ratio 1.004 at INT4, range 0.889 to 1.109). H-G7 is **not met** by its rule (the share of the language's mass carried by the group does not decline: median 0.048 in FP, 0.051 at INT4, 11 of 27 languages decline; the retained fraction of the group's peak declines in 26 of 27 languages but its median is not non-increasing, 0.922 / 0.906 / 0.922 at INT8 / INT7 / INT6, one member of 64).

Consequences, fixed before any intervention number:

1. **Fine up ratios.** The grid of section 3 has 1.41 as its smallest up ratio, too coarse to see a repair near the 1 / 0.852 = 1.17 the baseline suggests. Two ratios, **1.1 and 1.2**, are added to the calibration jobs and to every main job for the group and for all three controls. They are extra points, not part of the three chosen up ratios; they enter `alpha*`, H-G5a and H-G5b only. A main job then has 1 + 1 + 3 + (6 + 2) x 4 + 1 = **38** configurations per language and state (cost about 1.27 times the 30 of section 4).
2. **H-G5 is split in two** (the old H-G5 is replaced by these; its wording above stays as the record of the first version).
   - **H-G5a (generic repair).** At INT4 the median over languages of the harm of the *matched controls* (mean of the three draws) scaled by 1.1 or 1.2 is below 0, its 95 % interval (bootstrap over languages) excluding 0: scaling any 64 features up by about the INT4 shrinkage lowers the INT4 loss.
   - **H-G5b (group-specific repair).** At INT4 the net harm of the group (group minus mean control) at the better of 1.1 and 1.2 is below 0 with the 95 % interval below 0 in at least 14 of 27 languages.
   - Reading rule: if H-G5a holds and H-G5b does not, the repair is generic (the shrinkage matters, the group is not special); if H-G5b holds, the group carries more than its share of the effect.
3. **Prediction of the optimal gain.** `alpha*(l, INT4)` is expected near `1 / g_ratio(l, INT4)` (range 1.07 to 1.35 over the languages); at INT8 to INT5 `alpha*` is expected within one grid step of 1. The rank correlation rule of the old H-G5 stays (above 0.3).
4. The calibration (section 3) uses 16 + 2 = 18 ratios; the rule that picks the three down ratios is unchanged and ignores the two fine ratios.

## 11. Extension C: the repair near ratio 1 with a larger sample (written 2026-10-24 20:32, before any run of it; sections 1 to 10 unchanged)

**Why.** The first look at the main runs (D-074) saw, at INT4, a slightly negative net harm of the group scaled by 1.1 and 1.2 (loss falls) and a slightly positive one at FP; the per-language rule H-G5b (14 of 27 languages) was not met, and the audit (D-075) showed that the fine ratios are under-powered at 300 sentences and 3 control draws. A **post hoc, descriptive** look at the same data with the 27 languages as the units (not pre-registered, so only a motivation) gives: INT4 x1.1 median net harm -0.0010, 95 % interval over languages [-0.0022, -0.0005], negative in 21 of 27 languages (sign test p = 0.006); INT4 x1.2 median -0.0013 [-0.0034, +0.0004], negative in 18 of 27 (p = 0.12); FP x1.1 median +0.0007 [+0.0002, +0.0011], negative in 7 of 27; FP x1.2 +0.0020 [+0.0010, +0.0029], negative in 6 of 27. Extension C asks whether this sign change with precision is real, with a larger independent sample and a rule fixed now.

**Sample (never below the main study; only additions).** Text: the **562 reserve devtest sentences** (never used for any selection or evaluation; disjoint from the 300 evaluation and 150 pilot sentences), obtained from the unchanged runner as `--n-pilot 450 --n-eval 562 --split eval` (the hash order of the sentence ids does not depend on the sizes: `lm_ids(1012, 450, 562, 0)["eval"]` is exactly the old reserve); control draws: **all 5** of the manifest (draws 0 to 4); all 27 languages. Ratios: 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.7 (the first two are the primary ratios, the others locate the optimum). States: **INT4 and FP** (the contrast), then INT6 and INT5 if the slots allow (they test the dependence on precision). Cost: 4 ratios x (group + 5 controls) = 24 configurations plus the baseline over 829,000 tokens: about 3.6 GPU hours per state.

**Estimands.** Per language l and state s: the net harm of the group at ratio r, mean over the five control draws, pooled over the 562 sentences (`h_net`); language-level values are the units of the primary rules. Intervals: percentile intervals of the median over languages from a bootstrap over languages (5,000 draws, seed 0); the sentence and draw variation is inside the language-level values.

**Pre-registered rules (fixed before the runs; Holm over the two primary ratios 1.1 and 1.2, so the intervals are 97.5 %).**
- **H-Gc1 (repair at INT4).** At INT4, for at least one primary ratio the 97.5 % interval of the median net harm over languages lies entirely below 0 **and** at least 19 of 27 languages have a negative net harm (one-sided sign test p = 0.026). Met only if both hold for the same ratio.
- **H-Gc2 (the sign depends on precision).** At each primary ratio the median net harm at INT4 is below that at FP, the 97.5 % interval of the paired difference (INT4 minus FP, over languages) lies entirely below 0. - **H-Gc3 (generic or specific).** The same estimate for the matched controls (their own harm at the primary ratios): reported next to H-Gc1; the repair is called group-specific only if H-Gc1 is met and the 97.5 % interval of the median control harm at that ratio does not lie entirely below 0.
- **H-Gc4 (where the optimum lies).** Descriptive: median net harm and group harm at 1.1, 1.2, 1.4 and 1.7 at each state; the ratio with the lowest median group harm; the count of languages with `alpha*` at each ratio.

## 12. Extension D: does the optimal gain follow the quantization shrinkage? (written 2026-10-25 17:20, after the result of extension C (D-082) and before any number of this extension; sections 1 to 11 unchanged)

**Status:** post hoc in origin (suggested by the C result and by the baseline G1), exploratory, CPU only, no new job. **Question.** At INT4 the activation of a language's group on its own language is a factor g_l of the full-precision one (baseline G1, `g_ratio`: 0.743 to 0.934 over the 27 languages, median 0.852, so 1 / g_l between 1.07 and 1.35). If the small INT4 repair of extension C is compensation of that shrinkage, the ratio that minimises the group's harm should be larger for the languages whose group shrank more. The main study found no such relation (rank correlation of alpha* with 1 / g -0.034, D-078, 300 sentences, 3 draws, grid with the fine ratios 1.1 and 1.2 as its only ratios near 1); extension C has four ratios near 1 and 562 sentences.

**Data.** `gainc-4b` only (INT4, 562 reserve sentences, 27 languages): the pooled group harm per language at the ratios 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.7 (at ratio 1 the harm is 0 by definition); target tau_l = 1 / g_l of the INT4 row of `gain_study/baseline/baseline.csv`.

**Estimators of the optimal gain of language l.** *Grid (primary):* alpha_l = the ratio among {1, 1.1, 1.2, 1.4, 1.7} with the lowest group harm, equal values to the ratio nearest 1 (the definition of H-Gc4). *Smooth (secondary):* least-squares fit of harm(a) = c1 x + c2 x^2 with x = ln a, through the origin, over the four ratios; if c2 > 0 the vertex x* = -c1 / (2 c2) clipped to [0, ln 1.7], otherwise the end of [0, ln 1.7] with the lower fitted harm; alpha~_l = exp(x*).

**Rules (fixed now).**
- **R-D1 (the verdict).** Spearman rho(alpha_l, tau_l) over the 27 languages with a one-sided permutation p-value for rho > 0 (10,000 permutations, seed 0). *Supported* when rho >= 0.30 and p < 0.05 and the split-half reliability of R-D4 is at least 0.30; *uninformative* when that reliability is below 0.30 (not a refutation); *not supported* otherwise.
- **R-D2.** The same rank correlation with alpha~ (descriptive; no verdict).
- **R-D3 (placebo target).** rho(alpha_l, 1 / controls_ratio_l) (the shrinkage of the matched-control pool, `controls_ratio` of the baseline) and the difference rho(alpha_l, tau_l) - rho(alpha_l, tau^c_l) with a 95 % percentile interval from a bootstrap over languages (5,000 draws, seed 0); descriptive: a positive difference says that the group's own shrinkage tells more than the generic one.
- **R-D4 (reliability of alpha).** The 562 sentences are split by the parity of their position in the id list; alpha is computed on each half; the Spearman correlation between halves over the languages (rho_h), the Spearman-Brown reliability 2 rho_h / (1 + rho_h) of the full-sample alpha, and the attenuation-corrected R-D1 correlation rho / sqrt(reliability) (descriptive, capped at 1 in the report, not a verdict).
No other threshold. Everything is reported whatever the outcome.

**Limits.** Post hoc; 27 points; alpha on a grid of four ratios with steps of 0.1 to 0.3 against a spread of tau of about 0.06 (standard deviation), so a null does not exclude a weak relation; per-language harms of a thousandth of a nat are individually noisy (D-082 reliability table); one model, layer, SAE and quantizer; g_l is measured on the language's selection-independent text of the baseline, not on the 562 sentences.
